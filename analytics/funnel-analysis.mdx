Funnel analysis shows how shoppers progress through a sequence of steps — from discovery to purchase — and where they drop off. Use funnels to identify the biggest opportunities for conversion improvement.

## Default funnels

Luminos AI provides pre-built funnels that cover the core ecommerce journey:

### Purchase funnel

1. **Site visit** — any session on your storefront
2. **Product view** — viewed at least one product detail page
3. **Add to cart** — added at least one item to the cart
4. **Checkout started** — began the checkout process
5. **Purchase** — completed the order

### Search funnel

1. **Search performed** — entered a query in Smart Search
2. **Result clicked** — clicked a product from search results
3. **Add to cart** — added the clicked product to the cart
4. **Purchase** — bought the product

### Recommendation funnel

1. **Recommendation shown** — a recommendation widget was displayed
2. **Recommendation clicked** — clicked a recommended product
3. **Add to cart** — added the clicked product to the cart
4. **Purchase** — bought the product

## Creating a custom funnel

1. Go to **Analytics → Funnels → Create**
2. Name the funnel
3. Add steps in sequence — each step is an event type (page view, product view, add to cart, checkout, purchase, or custom event)
4. Optionally add filters to each step (e.g. only product views in a specific category)
5. Save and view the results

## Reading funnel charts

The funnel chart shows the number and percentage of shoppers at each step:

- **Step count** — how many shoppers reached this step
- **Conversion rate** — percentage of shoppers from the previous step who continued
- **Drop-off rate** — percentage who did not continue (1 - conversion rate)
- **Time between steps** — median time shoppers take to move from one step to the next

The widest gap between steps represents your biggest optimisation opportunity.

## Segmented funnels

Break down funnels by [customer segment](/segmentation/create-audience-segments), device type, traffic source, or geographic region. This reveals whether conversion gaps are universal or specific to certain audiences.

**Example:** If the add-to-cart to purchase drop-off is 70% on mobile but only 40% on desktop, the checkout experience may need mobile-specific optimisation.

<Tip>
Focus on the largest drop-off point first. Improving a 60% drop-off by 10 percentage points has far more impact than improving a 20% drop-off by the same amount.
</Tip>

## Date comparison

Compare funnel performance across time periods to measure the impact of changes. Select two date ranges and view the funnels side by side.
