Natural language search lets shoppers type queries the way they think — "red dress under 50 dollars for a summer wedding" — and get relevant results. Luminos AI parses the query into structured intent: product type, colour, price constraint, and occasion.

## How it works

When a shopper enters a natural language query, Luminos AI's NLP pipeline processes it in three stages:

1. **Intent classification** — determines whether the query is a product search, a question, or a navigation request
2. **Entity extraction** — identifies product attributes, price constraints, brand names, and contextual modifiers
3. **Query rewriting** — transforms the natural language input into a structured search query with the appropriate filters applied

For example, "comfortable black shoes for standing all day under 100" becomes a search for shoes with filters: colour=black, price≤100, and a relevance boost for products tagged with comfort-related attributes.

## Enabling natural language search

Go to **Search → Settings → Natural language** and toggle it on. Natural language processing runs as a pre-processing step before the standard search ranking pipeline, so it works alongside all your existing [synonyms](/search/synonyms-redirects), [merchandising rules](/search/search-merchandising), and [facets](/search/faceted-filtering).

## Supported query patterns

Luminos AI understands a wide range of conversational patterns:

- **Attribute filtering** — "blue wool sweater" → colour=blue, material=wool
- **Price constraints** — "under 50", "between 20 and 40", "around 30"
- **Size and fit** — "large men's jacket" → gender=men, size=L
- **Occasion** — "dress for a wedding" → occasion=formal (inferred)
- **Comparative** — "similar to [product name]" → triggers similar-item search
- **Negation** — "shoes not heels" → excludes heel-type footwear

## Accuracy and fallbacks

Natural language parsing is not perfect. When the engine is not confident in its interpretation, it falls back to standard keyword search. You can monitor parsing confidence scores under **Search → Analytics → NLP accuracy**.

<Tip>
Review misinterpreted queries weekly in the analytics dashboard. Adding [synonyms](/search/synonyms-redirects) for commonly misunderstood terms improves accuracy over time.
</Tip>

## Combining with visual search

Shoppers can upload an image through [visual search](/search/visual-search) and add a text modifier. For example, upload a photo of a dress and type "in blue" to find visually similar dresses in a different colour. The visual and text signals are merged in the ranking pipeline.

## Language support

Natural language search supports English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, and Japanese. The engine detects the query language automatically and applies language-specific parsing rules.
