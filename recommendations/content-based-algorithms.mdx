Content-based algorithms recommend products that share attributes with items a shopper has already viewed or purchased. Unlike collaborative filtering, they do not require data from other shoppers — making them especially useful for new stores and niche catalogs.

## How it works

Luminos AI builds a feature vector for every product in your catalog using:

- **Category and subcategory** — the product's position in your taxonomy
- **Brand** — manufacturer or designer
- **Price range** — bucketed into bands relative to the category median
- **Attributes** — colour, material, size, style tags, and any custom metadata you provide
- **Description embedding** — a semantic vector generated from the product title and description using a transformer model

When a shopper views a product, the engine finds the nearest neighbours in this feature space and ranks them by similarity score.

## Similarity metrics

Luminos AI supports two similarity metrics:

- **Cosine similarity** (default) — measures the angle between two product vectors. Works well for high-dimensional attribute spaces.
- **Euclidean distance** — measures the straight-line distance. Better when price proximity matters more than attribute alignment.

Switch between them under **Recommendations → Models → Content-based → Similarity metric**.

## Enriching your catalog

The quality of content-based recommendations depends directly on the richness of your product data. To get the best results:

- Add descriptive tags beyond basic categories — "boho", "minimalist", "outdoor" help the model distinguish styles
- Include material and composition data for fashion and home goods
- Use consistent naming conventions so the model can group related attributes

Review your catalog completeness under **Data → Product catalog → Health**. Products with fewer than three attributes are flagged as under-enriched.

<Tip>
Content-based algorithms are the best choice for "similar items" widgets on product detail pages, where the shopper's current interest is clear.
</Tip>

## Combining with collaborative filtering

You can run both models simultaneously. Create a hybrid placement under **Recommendations → Placements** and set the blend ratio — for example, 60% collaborative and 40% content-based. The engine merges the ranked lists and deduplicates before serving.

See [Collaborative filtering](/recommendations/collaborative-filtering) for details on the other half of the hybrid approach.
