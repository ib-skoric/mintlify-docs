Pricing schedules let you plan price changes in advance with automatic activation and deactivation. Use them for product launches, seasonal transitions, clearance cycles, and event-based pricing.

## How schedules work

A pricing schedule defines a series of price points with start dates. Luminos AI applies each price at the scheduled time and reverts to the next scheduled price when the period ends.

**Example: Product launch schedule**

| Date | Price | Phase |
|------|-------|-------|
| March 1 | £89.99 | Launch premium |
| March 15 | £79.99 | Standard price |
| June 1 | £69.99 | Mid-season reduction |
| September 1 | £49.99 | End-of-season clearance |

## Creating a schedule

1. Go to **Pricing → Schedules → Create**
2. Select the products (individual, category, or filtered set)
3. Add schedule entries with a date and target price (or percentage of base price)
4. Set the timezone for schedule activation
5. Save — schedules can be created months in advance

## Schedule types

### Markdown schedule

A series of price decreases over time, commonly used for fashion and seasonal goods. Each markdown brings the price closer to clearance level.

### Launch schedule

A high initial price that drops to a standard level after the launch period, then follows a normal pricing lifecycle.

### Recurring schedule

Prices that cycle on a weekly or monthly pattern. Example: 10% off every first Monday of the month, or premium pricing during weekends for event-related products.

## Interaction with other pricing features

Schedules set the **base price** for the scheduled period. Dynamic pricing rules, [competitor tracking](/pricing/competitor-tracking), and [promotional pricing](/pricing/promotional-pricing) can still adjust the actual selling price within the guardrails of the scheduled base price.

If a [promotional price](/pricing/promotional-pricing) is lower than the scheduled price, the promotion takes precedence during its active period.

<Tip>
Use schedules for planned lifecycle pricing and promotions for one-off discounts. Mixing the two keeps your pricing strategy clean and auditable.
</Tip>

## Calendar view

The **Pricing → Schedules → Calendar** view shows all scheduled price changes on a timeline. Use it to check for overlapping schedules that might create unintended prices and to coordinate pricing with your marketing calendar.

## Audit trail

Every scheduled price change is logged in the pricing audit trail under **Pricing → History**. The log shows the scheduled time, the previous price, the new price, and which schedule triggered the change.
