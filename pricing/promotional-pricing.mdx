Promotional pricing lets you create time-limited discounts, flash sales, and bundle offers that override your standard dynamic pricing rules for the duration of the promotion.

## Promotion types

### Percentage discount

Reduce the price by a fixed percentage. Example: 20% off all running shoes for one week.

### Fixed amount discount

Reduce the price by a fixed amount. Example: £10 off any order over £50.

### Bundle pricing

Set a special price when products are purchased together. Example: Buy a camera and lens together for £799 (saving £150 vs. buying separately).

### Buy X get Y

Offer a free or discounted item when a qualifying item is purchased. Example: Buy 2 shirts, get the 3rd at 50% off.

### Flash sale

A steep, time-limited discount that creates urgency. Flash sales typically last 2–24 hours with discounts of 30–60%.

## Creating a promotion

1. Go to **Pricing → Promotions → Create**
2. Select the promotion type
3. Define the scope — which products, categories, or the entire store
4. Set the discount value (percentage, amount, or bundle price)
5. Set the start and end date/time
6. Configure display options — whether to show a strikethrough original price, countdown timer, or savings badge
7. Save and schedule

## Promotion stacking

By default, promotions do not stack — only the highest-priority promotion applies to a product. If you want promotions to combine (e.g. a category sale plus a loyalty discount), enable stacking under **Pricing → Settings → Promotion stacking** and set the maximum combined discount percentage.

<Warning>
Set a maximum combined discount to prevent unintended deep discounts when multiple promotions stack. A cap of 40–50% is typical.
</Warning>

## Interaction with dynamic pricing

During an active promotion, the promotional price overrides dynamic pricing rules for affected products. Once the promotion ends, dynamic pricing resumes from the product's base price — not from the promotional price.

## Promotion analytics

Track promotion performance under [Pricing analytics](/pricing/pricing-analytics) → **Promotions**. Key metrics include:

- **Revenue during promotion** vs. the same period without a promotion
- **Units sold** — volume lift during the promotional period
- **Margin impact** — the trade-off between volume increase and per-unit margin decrease
- **Post-promotion dip** — whether demand drops below normal after the promotion ends (pull-forward effect)
